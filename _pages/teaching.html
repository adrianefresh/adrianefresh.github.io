TEACHING INTERESTS

I teach about institutions, policing and incarceration, research design, causal inference, and practical approaches to doing data science. I'm passionate about teaching students how to ask good questions, evaluate evidence, and build something of their own. This translates into my graduate mentoring as well. I try to ask questions that cultivate independent judgment, offer candid criticism, and attend to the person behind the work.
I also co-direct Duke's Social Science Scholars Program (SoSci Scholars), which supports undergraduate research across the social and behavioral disciplines. I've twice been awarded Top 5% Teaching Awards from Duke, and I also received the Centennial Teaching Assistant Award from Stanford while in my graduate program.
On a few occasions, I have been tasked with reflecting on how approach teaching. You can find an example of those reflections here: Teaching Statement.
