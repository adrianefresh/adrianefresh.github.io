WHAT I STUDY

How elites adapt to economic transformation
When does economic change displace established elites—and when do they capture the benefits for themselves? My research shows how Britain's expanding overseas trade was incorporated into existing parliamentary elites, and how the Industrial and Demographic Revolutions in Britain increased competitive pressures on elites, and hastened dynastic decline. I'm now using machine vision to assemble new historical data on where trade occurred from the Port Books, opening up questions about how the geography of economic change links to elite persistence.
How institutions are made and transformed
What drives institutions to expand or contract access and opportunity? I study moments of institutional reform— from voting rights expansions to electoral administration changes—to understand the conditions under which institutions become more or less inclusive. My work explores how institutions can simultaneously expand formal rights while maintaining substantive inequality through alternative mechanisms.
How exclusion changes when groups are threatened
My work shows how the political gains of Black people in the mid-century United States were not enough to prevent the rise of race-based incarceration, but also where exclusionary strategies appear absent: polling-place relocation does not systematically disadvantage non-copartisans in North Carolina in recent times. I’m now tracing [how federal funding built local police capacity, why some places received more, and what those investments changed over the short and long run]—bringing a historical perspective to debates over police funding.
What causal studies tell us about social phenomena
How can we infer causality when meaning emerges through interaction and interpretation? My work shows why assigning a conversational policy does not identify the effects of the messages it produces. I'm now developing [experiments that identify message effects within ongoing conversations] and [a theory of how social constructs operate through shared categorization and interpretation]—and what causal studies can establish about them.
