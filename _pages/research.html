WHAT I STUDY

How elites adapt to economic transformation
When does economic change displace established elites—and when does it strengthen them? My research shows how [Britain’s expanding overseas trade was incorporated into existing parliamentary elites rather than producing wholesale elite turnover]. I’m now using machine vision to assemble new historical data on where trade occurred, opening up questions about how its geography shaped political change.
How institutions are made and transformed
What drives institutions to expand or contract access and opportunity? I study moments of institutional reform— from voting rights expansions to electoral administration changes—to understand the conditions under which institutions become more or less inclusive. My work explores how institutions can simultaneously expand formal rights while maintaining substantive inequality through alternative mechanisms.
How exclusion changes when rights expand
When do challenges to social hierarchies become demands for greater social control? How do elites deploy institutions to maintain positions of advantage? And how do citizens interact with the state's social control institutions? I study how changes to the social order and perceived status threat shape policing, punishment, and incarceration. My research in this domain is especially interested in racial group status and carceral institutions in the United States.
What causal studies tell us about social phenomena
How can we infer causality when meaning emerges through interaction and interpretation? My work shows why assigning a conversational policy does not identify the effects of the messages it produces. I’m now developing [experiments that identify message effects within ongoing conversations] and [a theory of how social constructs operate through shared categorization and interpretation]—and what causal studies can establish about them.
